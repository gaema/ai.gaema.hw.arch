Matrix engines, compared by what they publish

Twelve of the thirteen parts have a matrix engine. The Radeon 610M does not. Of the twelve, the Arc A370M and the Blackhole p300c publish no peak. The other ten quote one, almost never for the same format and never at the same basis, and two of those quotes are only for the sparse case. This page puts each published rate beside the operand it was quoted for, the clock it was quoted at, and whether it is dense or 2:4-sparse — one chart per basis, because a chart that mixed them would rank these parts by which vendor quotes which number.

Quoted with no stated basis

A vendor headline figure that names no operand format, no accumulate width and no sparsity basis cannot be reconstructed, so it is not drawn as a bar beside figures that can be. It is quoted here instead, with what the vendor does and does not say about it.

PartAs printedFigureSource
GeForce RTX 5090Tensor Cores (AI) — 5th Generation, 3352 AI TOPS3,352 AI TOPSNVIDIA — GeForce RTX 5090 specifications · vendor-published
NVIDIA's GeForce specification page carries no footnote defining AI TOPS: it names no operand format, no accumulate width and no sparsity basis. The professional part on the same die publishes its comparable figure as “Theoretical FP4 TOPS using sparsity”, but that footnote is on the other page and is not this one's.
Arc Graphics 140VXe2 GPU — “delivering more than 60 tera-operations per second (TOPS)”60 TOPS (vendor floor)Intel — Lunar Lake architecture fact sheet (newsroom, June 2024) · vendor-published
Intel's Lunar Lake architecture fact sheet attributes this to the Xe2 GPU of the platform generally, and names no operand format, no accumulate width, no sparsity basis and no clock. It is quoted as a vendor floor (“more than 60”), not drawn as a bar: a figure nobody can reconstruct cannot be compared with one that can.

Every figure is a published peak — a ceiling, not a measurement. No performance measurement of ours appears here.
